# Terrain-matched fitted face trial

2026-09-29. This is an isolated exact-project Unity mirror trial on the generated canyon with seed `24681357` and canonical source ID `d1c85c86013ce2b97008b5c689c0d111`. The production scene, World Creator profile, material, shader, and dirty runtime files were not changed. The mirror source is evidence, not a ready-to-apply patch: it contains earlier canyon topology, contact-fitting, and clearance experiments plus temporary environment switches.

## Cause and visual result

The [original fitted-face control](TerrainMatchedFaceOriginalControl.png) has dark rectangular bars on the right wall. The earlier [face-and-Workbench control](FittedFaceAndWorkbenchControl.png) shows the same pattern. Disabling Workbench buttresses left those bars; disabling fitted faces removed them, while leaving the terrain's broad bands. The separate [terrain-only control](TerrainOnlyCliffControl.png) shows the underlying smooth, pale cliff. The bars come from the near-only fitted-face overlay rendered with the separate gray rock material. The small isolated dark stones come from the Workbench buttress pass. The [rim-outcrop trial](RimOutcropPairedCandidate.png) did not create the bars and affected only about 0.65% of RGB bytes in the paired overview; it is not selected for integration. Its [exact source](RimOutcropDecoratorTrial.cs.txt) and [determinism result](RimOutcropDeterminism.xml) are retained.

In the [terrain-matched candidate](TerrainMatchedFaceCandidate.png), the same fitted face meshes use the terrain blend material and material samples packed into vertex colors at absolute world coordinates. The dark rectangles disappear, leaving thin face/terrain contact lines. The [trial decorator](TerrainMatchedFaceDecoratorTrial.cs.txt), [copied material](TerrainMatchedFaceMaterialTrial.mat.txt), and [focused fixture source](TerrainMatchedFaceProofTests.cs.txt) reproduce the experiment. The copied material is a mirror-only duplicate of `Greybox_Terrain.mat`; a production implementation should share a controlled material contract rather than create an unmanaged duplicate. A direct height-band [shader trial](HeightBandShaderTrial.shader.txt) made repeated painted stripes in the [rejected render](HeightBandShaderRejected.png), so it was restored in the mirror.

This overview is still unlike the user's reference: the parent walls are smooth, the talus apron is slight, Workbench stones read as scattered pebbles, and the image does not have the reference's broken ledges, distant mesa composition, or atmospheric depth. The [rendered Player third-stop frame](TerrainMatchedFaceGameCameraStop2.png) remains a dark, narrow floor-and-shadow view. It cannot serve as visual acceptance for the production camera.

## Determinism, contact, streaming, and cost

The 49-chunk focused [determinism result](TerrainMatchedFaceDeterminism.xml) passed 1/1 with normal, reversed, and rebased builds. The temporary test hashes mesh vertices, indices, and 8-bit-quantized vertex colors; it reported the same IDs and 122 fitted faces, 131 stones, 102 talus objects, and 29 buttresses across orders. This is a fixture result, not save/load compatibility proof.

The focused [terrain-triangle contact result](TerrainMatchedFaceContact.xml) passed its diagnostic execution 1/1 over 21 faces. Among 7,560 tested face vertices, maximum positive surface separation was 0.300 m and 4,574 exceeded 0.25 m. At 2,520 triangle-interior samples, visual and collider maxima were each 0.992 m and p95 was 0.460 m; no sample exceeded 1 m. Thus material matching hides much of the overlay but does not make the visible and colliding face continuously conform to the rendered terrain. The thin contact lines remain visible. Continuous player and BigARM traversal have not been accepted.

A macOS full-content Development Player build succeeded. Two rendered 1034 × 546 runs used the same binary, fixed five-waypoint driver, seed, source fingerprint, and requested absolute route. The [candidate telemetry](TerrainMatchedFacePlayerCandidateTelemetry.log) and [original-material control telemetry](TerrainMatchedFacePlayerOriginalControlTelemetry.log) both finished centers `23/25/27/25/23`, had nine terrain colliders at settled stops, and reached 49/49 decorated chunks and 49/49 adaptive far tiles at later stops. The candidate reached stop two with 49 decorated chunks and 31/49 adaptive far tiles, while the original-material control had 30 decorated and 0/49 adaptive far tiles there. Their early frame windows therefore cover different amounts of unfinished streaming work. Candidate stop-two p99 was 175.30 ms versus 57.68 ms in control; later stop-three p99 was 9.28 ms versus 58.21 ms. One timing pair with unequal completion cannot attribute either difference to the material or establish an arrival-frame budget. The stop-two `camera-hit` names the camera controller itself at zero distance; the logged camera remains at its 25 m pose, so this diagnostic is not evidence of a generated rock obstruction.

## Integration gate

Keep this material treatment and all topology changes in the mirror. Before changing production assets, make the parent terrain profile and Workbench accents read as a connected mesa and talus system in both overview and actual Player camera; close the visible face/terrain contact; measure equal-state streaming and generation cost against an explicit budget; prove collision through continuous hands-on traversal; and decide topology, material/decoration identity, and save compatibility. Retain the existing first-area canyon policy until that cutover is approved.
