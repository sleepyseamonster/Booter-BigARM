# Canyon talus apron and Workbench density trial

2026-09-29. This trial used the isolated Unity `6000.4.0f1` mirror at `/tmp/booter-canyon-zDplUi`, seed `24681357`, and the same generated canyon segment `d1c85c86013ce2b97008b5c689c0d111`. The live dirty checkout, source Workbench meshes, production scene, and profile were not edited. The mirror already contains earlier unaccepted canyon changes; the two patches below are relative to that mirror state, not to production `main`.

## Terrain and rock candidates

The [apron query patch](ApronTerrainCandidate.patch) subtracts a smooth, segment-derived deposit from the canyon cut between normalized cross-section distances `0.14` and `0.48`, with a `0.06 × depth` peak and an endpoint fade. It widens the toe transition while retaining the generated segment's width, depth, and shelf controls. A stronger `0.10 × depth` trial reduced the 49-chunk source-linked talus count from 102 to 48, so it was reduced before the dense trial. The reduced apron alone returned 103 talus, 132 stones, 241 fitted faces, and 29 buttresses in the deterministic fixture.

The [Workbench debris patch](DenseWorkbenchDebrisRejected.patch) enlarged the first three existing source-rock variants to `3.5–5.5 m`, `1.8–3.0 m`, and `0.8–1.6 m` widths, and raised span admission from 20% to 45%. With the apron, the [focused suite](ApronDenseFocusedResults.xml) passed 15 of 15 checks. Its 49-chunk normal, reverse, and rebased runs each produced 241 fitted faces, 202 source-linked stones, 173 talus objects, and 29 buttresses with the same IDs and quantized geometry. The maximum talus center contact gap was 0.24 m with no poor center contacts. The smaller near-triangle contact fixture sampled 18 fitted faces: maximum positive vertex difference 0.150 m and all reported visual/collider face maxima below 0.50 m. This does not establish the same triangle-contact bound for every face in the 49 chunks, continuous collider contact, or manual traversal.

## Visual and Player result

The [fixed rim diagnostic](ApronDenseRimDiagnostic.png) shows a wider toe and several larger rocks, but the opposite wall remains a very large smooth slab. The render used temporary harness lighting and a fixed rim camera at `(429.12, 243.16)` with 26° pitch. The [actual game-camera frame](ApronDensePlayerStop2.png) is still a dark floor view. Neither meets the supplied image's layered escarpments, broken rock mass, foreground scale, and long-distance canyon composition.

A wall-aware shader projection trial removed some vertical texture streaks but did not give the slab geological structure; extending near detail and forcing a high rock signal on fitted faces also did not fix it. The [diagnostic](WallProjectionRejectedRim.png) records that direction. Those shader and face-color edits were reverted in the mirror and are not included in either candidate patch.

The [Development Player build](ApronDenseBuildResult.log) succeeded and its [full-content route log](ApronDensePlayerTelemetry.log) completed all five absolute waypoint placements at 1034 × 546. The fixed 25/15-second settle windows did not reach equal state with the preceding back-seam build: at stop three, 239 chunks were loaded, 36 were decorated, 13 decorations remained pending, and the far meshes measured 0/49 adaptive. The prior back-seam result had 49 decorated chunks and 49/49 adaptive far meshes at its equal-state comparison. This run therefore cannot support a settled frame-time comparison. Arrival windows include large frame spikes and intermittent Unity telemetry network failures; no causal cost is assigned solely to the larger rocks.

## Decision and next proof

Reject the 45% dense debris patch as a production setting. It adds 71 stones over the prior back-seam candidate across the same 49 chunks, does not achieve the visual target, and lacks equal-state Player cost proof. Keep the `0.06 × depth` apron as a mirror-only terrain candidate for a future wall-shape pass; it preserves the route and restores talus coverage after the stronger apron failed. The next implementation experiment should address terrain-scale wall morphology and material projection/strata together, then repeat whole-visible-wall contact, streaming settle, collision, deterministic reload/rebase, and comparable Player profiling. The user retains hands-on visual and traversal acceptance. A topology/material/decor version and save-compatibility decision is required before any production cutover.
