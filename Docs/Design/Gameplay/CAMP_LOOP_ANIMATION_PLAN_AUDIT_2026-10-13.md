# Camp Loop animation plan audit

Date: 2026-10-13. Audited revision: `9f891713136ef8336ddf2b0aab676a1ac897de7e`. Scope: source inspection, download accounting, dependency review and plan rewrites. Implementation is pending.

The first plans covered the requested objects and animation families, but left several integration boundaries implicit. The revised plans put the missing foundations ahead of the station features, separate source/preview/physical readiness, and require complete action sequences. The next implementation should begin with dependency restoration and scene/session safety, then target/input/action/motor contracts, rather than building twelve furnished stations first.

## Download and rig accounting

The historical five-folder inventory remains correct for its scope: 73 copies, 72 unique FBXs, one character and 71 motion files. The expanded scan adds 73 loose motion exports and two standalone character aliases, giving **148 task collection copies and 74 distinct byte payloads**. The extra two payloads are earlier sprint attempts, not two new usable animation choices. All three sprint versions have the same per-frame pose signature at six-decimal matrix precision and retain forward travel; this is not a claim of byte-identical FBXs or continuous Unity root-motion equivalence.

Retain 72 planned Unity FBX destinations, 68 unarmed candidates and three restricted imported references. Preserve the two additional sprint versions as local source history outside `Assets`. Separate original rig alternatives and the mesh-only upload input from this motion collection. The expanded [evidence](../../Evidence/Gameplay/AnimationPlayground20261013/PlanReaudit/expanded-source-inventory.json) and [rig/sprint probe](../../Evidence/Gameplay/AnimationPlayground20261013/PlanReaudit/rig-sprint-probe.json) preserve hashes and scope.

The original 74-bone character and accepted Mixamo character both have 31,739 vertices; both lack material slots. The 102-bone export has 51 meshes, 31,608 vertices and full finger-chain names. These facts make the original rig worth qualifying in Unity; they do not establish Humanoid validity, skin quality or grip. Copy From Other Avatar is appropriate for matching hierarchies. A fuller target rig needs its own mapped Avatar and Humanoid retarget validation, not the Mixamo source Avatar copied onto a different skeleton. [Unity Humanoid import](https://docs.unity3d.com/6000.4/Documentation/Manual/ConfiguringtheAvatar.html).

## What should be built first

The [foundation plan](./CAMP_LOOP_ANIMATION_FOUNDATIONS_PLAN_2026-10-13.md) specifies the prerequisite slices: private dependency/metadata restoration; scene/bootstrap and save/preference domains; shared target/activity contracts; router/modal integration; action clock and transaction ledger; owned motor/presentation/reset contracts; and a parameterized character/contact qualification fixture. These are narrow extensions of current owners. The repo already has Rigidbody movement, UI modes, gathering, inventory, camp, vitals, fixed terrain and tests; it does not need a new general game framework before this playground.

The [motion backlog](./CAMP_LOOP_ANIMATION_MOTION_BACKLOG_2026-10-13.md) prioritizes backward locomotion, standing turns, hold/carry/place, complete rest/seat transitions and posture-compatible reactions/death. Several jump/landing and start/stop roles can be derived or retargeted from current assets, so they are not automatically extra downloads. Mount/dismount and a standing wall/crowd alternative remain requested gaps. Lean/peek, unarmed defensive moves, item-use and environmental idle variants are later polish.
