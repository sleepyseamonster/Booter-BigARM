# Camp workstations and battery audit

Date: October 14, 2026. Scope: repository audit and design proposal for a camp workbench, dust refinery and chest in `CampLoopTesting`. The requested outcome is a playable preparation loop: craft an empty battery from chest supplies, harvest Arc Dust, dock the canister at the refinery, and charge the battery with Arc Energy. Implementation and recipe balance remain a subsequent author decision.

The existing interaction, inventory transactions, camp cache and harvester registry provide useful foundations. The largest gap is the canister handoff: pickup currently returns an empty tool to Booter and awards loose dust to Legger cargo. A refinery that physically accepts a filled canister needs carried contents and ownership preserved across that handoff. Crafting recipes, batteries and a gameplay refinery do not currently exist in the active runtime.

## Recommended first loop

1. Booter opens the camp chest and sees the exact ingredients for one empty battery. The workbench shows required and available quantities from that linked local chest.
2. Booter interacts with the workbench and selects **Craft Empty Battery**. One transaction removes all ingredients and creates one empty battery in an explicit destination, initially Booter's kit. Insufficient room or ingredients changes nothing.
3. Booter deploys the existing harvester. Pickup preserves the canister's actual dust rather than awarding it as loose dust. Empty canisters can remain personal tools; propose filled canisters travelling in nearby Legger cargo to preserve bulk-resource ownership. This needs content-aware policy rather than classifying hidden resources as ordinary tools.
4. At the refinery, Booter selects **Insert Canister** and **Insert Battery** from physically accessible owners. Two visible sockets show the selected contents and empty battery. Each insertion moves ownership into the station; it does not leave another usable copy in inventory.
5. **Refine and Charge** consumes the configured dust amount and converts the empty battery into a charged Arc Energy battery in one transaction. Keep the reusable canister and any excess dust. No loose Arc Energy stack is needed initially.
6. Booter retrieves the battery and canister. A full destination leaves them safely in their sockets. A charged battery in inventory is the first slice's endpoint; powering equipment is a later use case.

Use a single batch per button press initially. This avoids job queues, fuel and timer cancellation while establishing the actual crafting and conversion loop. A brief effect can follow the committed result. A timed refinery is a later alternative that needs reserved inputs, elapsed simulation time, cancellation, sleep acceleration and checkpoint rules.

## Battery recipe and starter chest proposals

There is no existing canonical battery recipe to stock. The following are two concrete options, not accepted economy values.

| Option | Empty battery recipe | Exact initial chest contents | Scope |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Minimal functional prototype | 4 Ironstone Ore -> 1 Empty Battery | 4 Ironstone Ore | Reuses an existing ingredient; treats battery assembly as a deliberately simplified recipe and leaves ore processing abstract. Fastest route to testing both stations. |
| More expressive salvage recipe | 4 Ironstone Ore + 2 Conductive Salvage + 1 Insulating Sleeve -> 1 Empty Battery | 4 ore, 2 salvage, 1 sleeve | Communicates casing, conductor and insulation. Adds two item definitions and eventually needs renewable acquisition routes beyond the starter chest. |

Start with the minimal option unless the author wants those material distinctions now. Keep the workbench extensible through recipe assets so equipment recipes can follow without building a full crafting tree first. Dust belongs to charging at the refinery, not to the empty battery recipe.

For an initial charging comparison, propose **10 dust + 1 empty battery -> 1 charged battery**, retaining the canister and leftover contents. At the current collection rate, the first charge needs 100 simulation seconds and a full canister supplies ten charges if enough empty batteries exist. These are prototype units, not a physical energy model. Requiring all 100 dust would make the first attempt take about 16 minutes 40 seconds; partial-canister use provides a much shorter first loop.

Derive the chest's seed quantities from the selected battery recipe asset multiplied by a configured starting batch count of one. Validate item IDs, positive quantities and storage capacity before granting anything. Avoid an independently typed chest list that can drift when the recipe changes. The initial chest holds crafting ingredients only; battery and canister storage becomes available through its broader storage policy.

## Implementation boundaries

**Reuse existing inventory transactions.** Add recipe definitions and a craft service inside `BooterBigArm.TopDown3D.Runtime`. Prepare drafts for every participating inventory, remove ingredients, validate output space after removal, recheck station/local-source access and revisions, then install all drafts before notifications. Never sequence independent live removes and adds. Station state and canister payload mutations need coordinated validation and commitment too; inventory drafts alone do not make external state atomic.

**Preserve carried contents.** Add a bounded canister instance record containing a persistent instance ID, integer dust, capacity/version and fractional collection progress where applicable. Give every instance exactly one owner: inventory slot, deployed placement or refinery socket. Extend transfer, move/swap, drafts, packing, mass and snapshot validation to preserve it; do not use slot index or Unity instance ID as identity. Make stateful canisters nonstackable. Partial canisters must also be redeployable from accessible nearby cargo, preserving dust and progress; otherwise retaining leftovers would strand them outside Booter's deployment path. Existing empty tool stacks need an explicit lossless conversion into empty instances before this goes to production. Existing loose dust remains valid; offer an explicit refinery loose-dust input or separately reviewed migration, never silently discard it or invent historical contents.

**Keep the first battery simple.** Separate empty and charged item definitions can represent a binary state without a general charge simulation. If subsequent use requires partial charge, add battery instance identity and charge units then. Require valid icons and carry metadata for every new definition.

**Extend the existing camp cache deliberately.** Prefer evolving the one cache into camp storage with a separate authored storage policy, preserving its GUID, stable identity, capacity and existing resource snapshots. Update the currently resource-only UI and transfer rules together. Use scene-specific starter recipe configuration in the camp loop; do not seed the shared production prefab by accident. A second chest would require changing the existing one-cache assertions and the save service's single-cache discovery/DTO contract.

**Keep station behavior separate from recovery camp behavior.** Workbench/refinery belong to the established site, not the portable cloak or Legger. Use authored prefabs under `Assets/_Project/Prefabs/TopDown3D/Camping/`, station scripts under `Scripts/Runtime/TopDown3D/Camping/`, and recipe settings under `Settings/Items/`. These are proposed destinations. Reuse Input System actions, binding labels, shared HUD/EventSystem and current gameplay suppression. Close or invalidate a station action when access, posture, actor state or scene ownership changes.

**Add only owned scene objects.** Furnish the camp with a work surface, a refinery with two docking sockets, and the existing chest nearby. Survey ground, approach clearance and the seven-rock camp enclosure before choosing coordinates. Keep Legger's unloading space, the return corridor, camp recovery space and Animation Playground routes usable. Primitive visuals are enough to qualify behavior; final art and contact animations remain separate acceptance work. Do not invoke the scene builder's Build/Complete methods on this furnished scene.

## Identity and persistence contract

| Concern | Proposed contract |
| --- | --- |
| Deterministic world identity | Use the existing authored world identity `greater-wasteland.authored.v1`. Recipes and stations have explicit versioned IDs. Neither crafting nor refining changes terrain or population generation. |
| Stable object identity | Assign authored workbench/refinery IDs and retain the existing chest ID. Canister instance identity persists through pickup, placement, cargo, storage and docking; placement address is separate from instance identity. |
| Streaming and unload | Terrain streaming is not applicable: Greater Wasteland and the lab use fixed loaded terrain. Scene unload still releases UI, effects and subscriptions. Reconstruct lab defaults once per new session; later production restoration reconstructs exact owners and socket contents. Future dressing-cell visibility must not own inventory or station state. |
| Authored constraints | Stations remain at the established camp with supported ground, legal collision and reachable approach points. Inventory access never reaches distant camps or an absent Legger. |
| Runtime deltas | In production, checkpoint chest depletion/seed grant state, canister contents/ownership, refinery sockets and any future active job together with kit/cargo. Crafting inputs and outputs must never load from different moments. Extend snapshot versions, validation, writer revision tracking and safe-save gates together. |
| Lab persistence | Preserve current session-only policy. Seed once per session; explicit loop reset restores the chest, actors' test inventories, placements and refinery sockets together. Merely closing UI does not refund inputs or replenish supplies. Ordinary animation-station resets should not reset the economy. |

## Build sequence and proof

1. **Item and recipe contract:** select recipe/charging values; add battery definitions and pure crafting transaction behavior. Verify exact consumption, missing ingredients, blocked output, multiple ingredient sources, stale revisions and observer failure after commit.
2. **Canister continuity:** preserve instance contents through placement/pickup/transfer; verify two differently filled canisters, partial progress, cargo reach, nonstacking, single ownership and snapshot round trips. Retain the current placement limit and no-offline-progress behavior.
3. **Camp storage:** expand policy/UI, seed from the chosen recipe once, and verify resources still stay out of Booter's kit, equipment can transfer locally, repeated opening/install does not refill, and reset restores one complete baseline.
4. **Workbench and refinery:** add dispatch, modal screens and sockets; verify wrong/empty inputs, insufficient dust, exact leftovers, insertion/retrieval capacity failures, double submit, access lost during action, disable/unload and atomic ownership changes.
5. **Camp loop integration:** add only owned station content and scene-specific seed configuration. Validate the nine terrain/collider pairs, twelve nodes, original cache GUID/ID, actors, routes and lab marker. Preserve production scene/assets and checkpoint files. Tests that open or clear scenes run only in an isolated hydrated candidate.
6. **Production persistence later:** migrate old empty-canister stacks and loose dust safely; validate the expanded coordinated checkpoint before any state applies. Refuse incompatible data without overwriting recovery files. Do not install generated-world save services.
