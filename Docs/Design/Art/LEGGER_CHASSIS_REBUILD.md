# Legger original frame audit and chassis rebuild

October 16, 2026. The author requested an original-frame audit followed by implementation in the same native scene. **Audit and rebuild complete; the [chassis checkpoint](../../Evidence/Art/LeggerChassis20261016/README.md) records final native verification.** The retained dense original controls the design language; the accepted mirrored three-section legs, chosen scale, rider interfaces, cargo and body panels constrain its fit. The saved model has 75 major meshes, 42,552 quad control faces and 136,928 evaluated triangles. This authoring budget is not measured Unity performance or final creative approval.

## Audit findings

Fourteen fresh calibrated views compare the dense original, current bare frame/drive and assembled body. Original side, oblique, rear, top and underside views are supplemented by a half-side camera cut and a central slab at X=±0.18 m. These expose occluded structure without modifying or saving the source. Clipped surfaces are inspection cuts, not real part boundaries. The generated original is fused and has no verified mechanical assembly or rig.

The original's principal side feature is a broad cast diagonal descending rearward from the front neck toward the footrest junction. A compact forward yoke, tall tapered footrest support and layered saddle rails form a purposeful mechanical hierarchy. The central drive mass sits farther inboard/rearward, leaving readable space around the upper leg. Curved hoses/support routing hangs beneath the rear body and cargo. The current continuous deep cradle, wide forward drive pods and low horizontal footrests obscure these relationships. Extra bevels on that cradle cannot recover the original design.

Calibrated original-side readings provide approximate Y/Z design landmarks: neck around (−0.39, 1.94), diagonal shoulder around (−0.35, 1.78), rear diagonal junction around (0.00, 1.59), front yoke bearing around (−0.54, 1.69), and upright footrest web from about (−0.04, 1.80) to (0.03, 1.53), in metres. These covered/form landmarks have roughly 20–30 mm reading uncertainty. They are neither exact bearing axes nor complete cross sections. The diagonal's approximately 29° downward rearward side slope is a silhouette guide. Preserve the current real steering bearing and leg joints when adapting these forms.

## Implementation brief

- Replace the continuous deep front/bottom cradle with two tapered neck-to-footrest cast diagonals, restrained upper/rear rails and fitted crossmembers. Keep open spaces around the upper leg and drive unit.
- Add one compact forward yoke per side, with nested bearing shapes and a tapered lower guard. These are major silhouette parts, not separate fasteners.
- Rebuild hip mounts around the accepted hip axes, without moving either leg. Connect them purposefully into the diagonal/crossmember structure.
- Recover tall tapered footrest webs while retaining the current pad/contact position. Follow the saddle pan with shaped support rails; retain the actual steering bearing and grip position.
- Compact the five drive/cooling housings because their broad forward mass currently fills the desired frame openings. Keep them independently editable in the existing Drive module. Retain cargo rack geometry and all cargo parts; refit service routing into deliberate rear arcs.
- Build quad control surfaces along the swept cast sections, closed panels and housings. Keep silhouettes and deep openings in geometry; bake shallow relief and restrained wear into new Frame/Drive atlases. Retain the palette and all other module textures.
- Compare original/before/after in side and oblique views, then inspect bare structure, underside, rear, front, top and the fitted complete model. Verify source preservation, unchanged leg/rider/cargo fingerprints, UVs/maps, topology, bounds and independent native reopening before applying the candidate.
