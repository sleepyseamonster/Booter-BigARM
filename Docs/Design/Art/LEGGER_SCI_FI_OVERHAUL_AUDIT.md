# Legger form audit and sci-fi overhaul brief

October 15, 2026. **Audit complete; the requested visual overhaul has not yet been applied.** The current modular rebuild is a usable technical baseline, but its major forms do not meet the author's visual direction. The author finds the crates the strongest remaining area and requests an overhaul of the rest toward the original sleek, futuristic sci-fi design. This supersedes the earlier plan's instruction to preserve the six existing body fairing shapes. It does not supersede the chosen scale, one-scene organization, editable major parts or ivory/dark-metal/brown-leather palette with restrained wear.

The original design is already retained inside the native file. It is the primary reference for this work; an unrelated cyberpunk concept is unnecessary. The audit compared actual saved meshes, including clay renders that remove their different texture treatments. [Evidence and source identity](../../Evidence/Art/LeggerSciFiAudit20261015/README.md) accompany this brief. Detailed shape treatments below are modeling recommendations implementing the requested direction, not approval of an unseen final candidate.

## Finding

The rebuild preserved broad dimensions and a clean component inventory while replacing designed surfaces with generic primitives. The loss remains conspicuous in clay and in front, side and elevated views. More surface scratches, darker textures or another polygon-reduction pass will not recover the missing volumes.

The original combines a compact motorcycle-like body with crowned armor, swept edges, nested mechanical housings, a scooped ribbed saddle, and arched split feet. Ivory plates wrap around dark structure and taper into their joints. The rebuild uses nearly flat strips, straight cores, large unrelieved joint disks, rectangular housings and a long thin seat ramp. These are changes to large and medium forms, not merely losses of tiny source detail.

The audit does not establish that every original bump is intentional or mechanically meaningful. Reconstruct its coherent contours and assembly hierarchy while removing generated lumps, ambiguous joins and fused hardware.

## Form construction rules

Model the silhouette and cross-section first. Trace the reference's side profile, front width and elevated shoulder line before adding service seams. Establish an intentional transition from a broad, gently crowned surface into a narrower edge or joint recess. A uniform bevel applied to a box is insufficient for these parts.

Use editable quad strips or lofts whose loops follow ridges, bend transitions, perimeter lips and joint openings. Keep sensible spacing over broad areas, then add loops where curvature needs them. Equal square polygons everywhere would spend faces on flat areas and constrain the desired forms. Maintain deliberate hard/soft normal boundaries; inspect edge-on views so smooth shading does not disguise flat geometry. Use a shared local limb construction frame for left/right design consistency, then fit each chain to its different saved pose.

Keep large gaps, undercuts, shell thickness, overlapping lips and anything that changes the outline in geometry. Bake stitching, grain, tiny fasteners, shallow panel relief, wear and small cooling-fin relief after the surfaces are established. Deep openings still need geometry and an interior; a dark texture alone will not read correctly from another angle. Do not shrinkwrap the entire new cage onto every noisy original vertex or indiscriminately rebake the original generated surface.

Retain the current palette and restrained wear. The author clarified that polished means better geometry, not a factory-new glossy finish. Restore the original balance of ivory upper shells over dark mechanical recesses with selected warm hardware/leather accents. Materials should describe the rebuilt forms rather than conceal them. No new cloth, wood, biological source or material-crafting rule is introduced by this brief; retained leather appearance follows the existing specific author direction and does not establish a plentiful world resource.

## Scale, interfaces and budget

The saved rebuild measures approximately **0.866 × 1.5965 × 2.226 m**. The dense original at its saved scale measures **0.8586 × 1.6463 × 2.2277 m**. Their heights differ by about 1.7 mm. Gross scale is therefore not the cause of the visual regression. Retain the user's chosen scale, ground plane, case arrangement and rider interfaces as starting constraints.

Preserving a rebuilt joint center is not proof that it matches the original design. Record reference landmarks and the current hip/knee/ankle centers before shaping their new housings. Prefer recovering the desired form around the current centers. If an interface change is needed, document its position and clearance effects explicitly rather than silently moving it to force a profile match. No rider-fit or articulation clearance is certified by this audit.

The current 86,072 evaluated triangles include 19,252 for frame, 6,544 for drive, 22,512 for both legs, **only 2,888 for body armor**, 3,452 for saddle, 4,552 for steering and 26,872 for cargo. Body armor has only 324 of the 21,396 control quads. The allocation does not reflect the importance of its visible surfaces. More important than a new global count is directing geometry into crowns, shoulders, curved edges and joint transitions, while reducing unnecessary sampling on thin round rails and tiny fixed fittings.

Stop further decimation while the design is being restored. A provisional reconstructed LOD0 envelope is roughly **96,000–120,000 evaluated triangles**, including the retained 26,872 cargo triangles. A possible allocation is frame/drive 20–26k, body 12–18k, legs 30–38k, saddle 3–5k and steering 4–6k. These are planning ranges, not a required increase or a measured Unity performance limit. Measure each candidate with all render/export modifiers evaluated. Runtime LODs and texture/material consolidation follow visual restoration.

## Implementation order and review checks

1. **Close the technical baseline — complete.** Keep the one active native file and organized assembly. Preserve the original and accepted technical checkpoints for comparison. Mark this rebuild as an art baseline requiring overhaul; do not treat earlier topology/bake checks as final visual acceptance.
2. **Restore the whole silhouette.** Shape body cheeks, upper shrouds, saddle envelope and limb/foot contours together in a simple neutral material. Use the existing crates to judge proportion. Compare the whole machine from front, side, three-quarter and elevated views before finishing an isolated part. Check the same views at smaller image sizes as readability proxies; actual Unity framing still needs its own review.
3. **Resolve the body and mechanical core.** Finish crown, waist, shoulder and overlap relationships, then fit the shaped frame, drive housings and hip interfaces beneath them. Remove the current pointed shroud gap as part of deliberate panel construction. Check bare structure and assembled appearance.
4. **Resolve both leg chains and feet.** Finish variable shell sections, layered joint covers, cast cores, actuators and swept feet, fitted to each side's pose. Review transitions through the entire chain. Keep articulation boundaries and future useful pivots separate.
5. **Resolve rider interfaces.** Finish the sculpted saddle/pan, integrated steering and compact controls, checking handlebar, knee and cargo clearances. Refine retained cargo only where assembly fit or the overall design requires it.
6. **Rebuild affected UVs and textures.** Existing atlases cannot automatically follow substantially remodeled surfaces. Create/pad the new islands, bake the revised high-detail sources into the module sets, preserve useful cargo maps, and inspect normal direction, seams, albedo/roughness separation and actual evaluated coverage. Keep material coordinates from hidden source copies out of visible UV0.
7. **Review and replace the same saved source.** Inspect original/current/candidate in matched textured and clay views. Check outline, major surface curvature, normal shading and readable mechanical layers before any polygon reduction. Re-run topology, evaluated budget, transforms/pivots, UV/map packing, reference preservation and saved-native reproduction checks for changed parts. Preserve a local recovery checkpoint, apply the verified candidate to the same authoring file, and keep the complete assembly as its default view.

These are execution and self-review checks, not additional requests for user permission. The author has requested the overhaul; final creative acceptance remains with the author after a concrete candidate exists. This audit completes the requested audit goal. It does not claim that steps 2–7 have been modeled, baked or saved.
