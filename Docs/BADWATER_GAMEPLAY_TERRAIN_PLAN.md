# Badwater gameplay terrain plan

**Status:** Implementation in progress, 2026-10-06. The dedicated Development Player builds from an isolated copy of the current working tree; traversal feel, close-range material quality, and Player performance are unverified. See the [baseline evidence](./Evidence/Badwater/gameplay-terrain-baseline-2026-10-06.md).

## Goal and boundary

Make the existing four-slice Badwater scene a reliable **playable geographic terrain study** with Booter and Legger, while keeping its measured elevation and tile identities. The entire 4.096 × 4.096 km terrain must remain explorable; close-range game-world quality is a separate art and traversal gate. This bounded scene is not a replacement for the effectively infinite production World Creator. The production scene, generator, and save system remain separate until a later integration decision.

The current [scene record](./Agents/Blender/studies/DeathValley/UNITY_BADWATER_SCENE.md) is the source for geographic bounds, source data, validation, and current limits. [WORLD_SYSTEMS_STANDARD.md](./WORLD_SYSTEMS_STANDARD.md) controls deterministic identity, chunk life cycle, collision readiness, near/middle/far representation, and saved deltas. [TOP_DOWN_3D_LANDSCAPE_BENCHMARK.md](./TOP_DOWN_3D_LANDSCAPE_BENCHMARK.md) supplies the existing 2 ms per-frame terrain work guardrail. Live scene assets and a Development Player profile decide performance claims.

## Audit of the earlier streaming recommendation

- **Confirmed:** The scene has 256 separate 256 m Unity Terrain objects and 256 TerrainColliders. Four adjoining focus tiles use 1 m samples; the other 252 use 2 m samples. All are currently loaded together. The four focus tiles are a *resolution tier*, not the only four terrain objects.
- **Correction:** A count of 256 tiles does not prove that full asset streaming is needed. Unity Terrain already reduces distant geometry according to its terrain settings. The saved scene uses a heightmap pixel error of 8 and instanced drawing, but no Development Player frame-time, memory, or boundary-crossing measurement has established its actual cost.
- **Correction:** Disabling renderers or colliders does not unload the TerrainData and textures referenced by this scene. True asset-memory streaming requires an asset ownership/loading boundary and an unloadable dependency path; dividing this 4 km scene into 256 additive scenes is not the first choice.
- **Risk:** Keeping all 256 colliders active may be unnecessary for gameplay, while switching them off without a prepared collision ring could drop Booter or Legger at a tile edge. The existing production world has collision-readiness logic; the Badwater scene does not.
- **Quality limit:** Only a 512 × 512 m focus area has 1 m height spacing. The other tiles use 2 m display spacing, and the geographic color tiles are 128 × 128 pixels across 256 m. Neither a detailed-looking aerial image nor a heightfield proves close-range ground material quality, ledges, overhangs, or safe routes.
- **Build gap:** Badwater is outside enabled Build Settings. The existing general build command takes enabled scenes, so an ordinary production build will not profile this scene. A dedicated, isolated scene-list build is required; do not change production Build Settings to obtain a baseline.

## Chosen method

Use a **profile-first, staged hybrid** for this bounded slice. Keep the complete terrain visible and its source TerrainData unchanged for now. Establish a standalone Development Player baseline and review representative close terrain and traversable routes. If collision has a material cost, activate colliders around **both** Booter and Legger with prefetch, a ready-before-crossing rule, and unload hysteresis. Do not disable distant visual terrain merely to reduce object count; the valley and mountain horizon is part of the requested experience. Escalate to true asynchronous asset streaming only if measured memory, startup, or traversal cost still fails the target after narrower terrain and collision tuning, or when the geographic area grows substantially. Improve close materials and route-defining geometry as separate quality work without changing recorded DEM elevations.

For eventual production integration, preserve `rNN_cNN`, source bounds, and source-version identity as immutable geographic inputs. Attach any generated content and mutable state to stable world/chunk IDs, with saved deltas outside TerrainData and scene objects. Use the production near/middle/far world-query and streaming path rather than introducing a parallel gameplay world manager in the Badwater scene.

## Implementation sequence and gates

| Step | Work | Proof and decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Define and capture the baseline** | Record target hardware, build target, quality/resolution, camera path and 8 km far clip, and provisional frame-time, memory, and startup budgets **before** tuning. Build a Development Player with Badwater as the explicit scene list in an isolated project copy; leave production Build Settings untouched. Measure cold startup, basin and mountain views, traversing ordinary and focus-tile borders, CPU/GPU frame-time distributions and spikes, memory, draw calls, and active terrain/collider counts. | A build is not a playtest. User-owned interactive traversal or separately authorized hands-on testing supplies the camera path and feel evidence. Record cold and repeat runs and distinguish Editor from Player cost. If no measured Player evidence is available, do not claim a streaming decision has been proven. |
| **2. Terrain and traversal quality gate** | Compare source hillshade and the Unity scene at selected survey coordinates and across all four quarters. Review basin floor, slopes, ridge and canyon approaches, tile borders, and Booter/Legger route safety. Check the close material at walking distance; use geographic imagery as color/mask reference and tileable game materials for ground detail. Keep DEM heights immutable and version any later authored collision/ledge geometry separately. | Record visual captures, sampled height and seam checks, material resolution/tiling, slope and step failures, and routes the Legger cannot use. Do not call the full 4 km game-world quality merely because four tiles have 1 m samples. Interactive appearance and traversal acceptance belong to the user. |
| **3. Narrow performance tuning, conditional** | If the baseline misses its recorded budget, test Terrain LOD, basemap, geographic texture resolution/import settings, the long camera view, and other measured hotspots one variable at a time. Preserve geographic reference and DEM samples. Compare identical fixed views and Player profiles. | Accept only a measurable improvement without visible holes, popping, or materially degraded horizon or close material. Revert settings that fail. Avoid claiming that active-collider count alone proves a physics bottleneck. |
| **4. Collision window, conditional** | Only if physics or startup profiling identifies collider cost, add a Badwater-specific runtime controller over existing tile IDs. Pre-enable neighborhoods around both actors before movement reaches an edge; retain a wider unload radius. Never retire a tile needed by either actor or an active route. Spawn/restore must start on ready collision. When collision is missing, movement waits at the last safe location; the Legger is never recalled or teleported to Booter. | Focused non-gameplay checks verify tile lookup, both actors' union, boundary/corner crossings, enable-before-disable order, stable IDs, and repeatability. User-owned traversal checks no falling, stalls, or false routes. The Player profile must show a real gain without activation spikes. |
| **5. Asset-streaming gate, conditional** | If memory/startup or expanded coverage still fails, build a versioned tile catalog from the source manifest and load terrain content asynchronously in bounded regions. Keep a coarse visible fallback until fine content and required collision are ready; use prefetch and unload hysteresis. Choose an asset delivery method after measuring package and dependency cost. | A cold Player run shows lower peak/steady memory or startup cost, no holes or collision gaps at crossings, no orphaned assets after unload, and stable source IDs. The existing 2 ms/frame terrain-work guardrail remains unless a controlled profile justifies a change. |
| **6. Production-world decision** | Decide whether the geographic slice stays a separate reference/playable scene or becomes a bounded authored influence in World Creator. Map source elevation, geography, materials, traversal constraints, and stable IDs to the production world-query contract. | Seed/location replay, chunk unload/reload, Booter and Legger traversal, near/far agreement, and save/restore of runtime deltas are proven before calling it production gameplay terrain. No change to first-playable geography is implicit. |

## Stop conditions and exclusions

The **loading decision** stops after Step 1 if the bounded scene meets its recorded performance budgets; Step 2 remains necessary before calling the terrain game-world quality. Do not add Addressables, hundreds of additive scenes, a second world generator, rock scatter, terrain resampling, or broad project-setting changes merely to make the tile count smaller. Steps 3–5 happen only for a demonstrated bottleneck. Step 6 requires its own integration review because the geographic study and production world have different ownership and persistence models.

This plan does not claim current Play Mode feel or Player performance. Repository and isolated-editor validation established scene structure, terrain seams, and geographic source alignment; interactive traversal and visual acceptance remain user-owned under the repository working agreement.
