# Stylized realism art direction research

October 15, 2026. Sable's research for the author of Booter & BigARM.

The author selected **stylized realism**, centered on Arcane and No Rest for the Wicked, with a stronger grounding in realism and a hand-painted appearance. Wayfinder and Ori and the Blind Forest are additional named influences. The strongest starting direction is believable construction, weight, and material identity, expressed through simplified shapes, authored brushwork, and composed light.

This research establishes a reference library and proposes how to combine those influences. Exact proportions, palette accents, brush visibility, and rendering treatment remain open. Production assets and settings are outside this research batch.

Read the [resource catalogue](RESOURCE_CATALOGUE.md) for direct sources, artists, talks, books, material libraries, and access notes. The [comparison study brief](COMPARISON_STUDY.md) turns the findings into a bounded future art exercise.

This accepted surface direction supersedes the open choice between the three study candidates; exact asset treatments still need refinement.

## How the named influences contribute

| Influence | Contribution to the proposed direction | What needs restraint |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arcane | Painted surface variation, designed planes, selective sharpness, cinematic staging, and believable wear | Shot-specific compositing and extreme close-up polish cannot establish how a playable 3D scene will read |
| No Rest for the Wicked | Painted forms and strong light grouping demonstrated from an elevated gameplay view | Keep anatomy and equipment closer to believable proportions than its deliberate exaggeration |
| Wayfinder | Clear silhouettes, economical shape families, coherent props, and a bridge between painting and lit materials | Reduce oversized heroic equipment and the broad comic emphasis where they undermine vulnerability |
| Ori and the Blind Forest | Depth through layers, controlled focal brightness, color relationships, and atmosphere | Translate these principles into barren terrain; vegetation, free water, and a forest palette do not transfer into game canon |

Fortiche describes a deliberate preference for handcrafted imperfection and confirms the combination of 3D production with a painted aesthetic. This supports treating painting as part of asset design, rather than relying on a final image filter. [Fortiche FAQ](https://forticheprod.com/faq/), [official artbook overview](https://forticheprod.com/blog/the-art-and-making-of-arcane-artbook-arcane/).

Moon's art directors describe concept projection followed by hand painting, refinement against the in-game camera, and custom rendering. They cite Caravaggio, Shishkin, Kuindzhi, and Levitan; they also explain that larger hands and emphasized faces were intentional. Its fixed camera supports compositions that cannot simply be assumed for another game's camera. [Moon Studios interview](https://80.lv/articles/interview-inside-the-tech-and-artistic-inspirations-of-no-rest-for-the-wicked-from-moon-studios).

Wayfinder's principal environment artist describes early development as combining Battle Chasers painterliness, Joe Madureira and Baldi Konijn's shapes, and Darksiders material rendering. These early experiments are references in their own right, not evidence that every demonstrated feature shipped. [Romain Durand's development retrospective](https://www.romaindurand.com/news/early-wayfinder-part-1).

## Painting references for realism and atmosphere

| Painter or source | Suggested lesson | Translation into the game |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Caravaggio | Large light and shadow groups; focal emphasis | Compose readable pools of light without allowing darkness to hide traversable ground or hazards |
| John Singer Sargent, especially Simplon Pass | Rock masses, selective edges, economical brushwork | Let a few broad plane changes carry a rock before adding grain and cracks |
| Arkhip Kuindzhi | Luminous sky against restrained land masses | Separate the orange sky from layered terrain through value and temperature rather than saturation everywhere |
| Isaac Levitan | Quiet atmosphere and grouped distance | Give the wasteland depth without filling its emptiness with decorative clutter |
| Death Valley geology and photographs | Ground truth for scale, fracture, strata, and weathered surfaces | Preserve recognizable physical forms while choosing which detail survives the gameplay view |

The National Gallery of Art's account of Sargent's **Simplon Pass** emphasizes strong masses of light and shade, visible brushwork, and a drier middle distance. It is a particularly practical painting reference for this project's rocks. Study its treatment of form; its vegetation, water, and sky color are outside the game's setting. [Collection record and image](https://www.nga.gov/artworks/166471-simplon-pass).

Museum sources for the other painting references are in the catalogue. Earth geology supplies visual reference, not new world rules: ancient dry channels can inform form, but ongoing rainfall and fluvial processes cannot be silently introduced into the Broken World. [NPS Death Valley geology](https://home.nps.gov/deva/learn/nature/geology.htm), [canonical world basis](../../../../WORLD_BASIS.md).

## Proposed visual principles

**Believable structure carries the realism.** Give metal thickness, stone convincing fracture, loads plausible support, and tools clear assembly. Keep anatomy and primary proportions grounded. Enlarge or simplify selected features only when a gameplay view demonstrates the need.

**Brushwork should describe form.** On rock, strokes can follow strata, planar breaks, and worn surfaces. On metal, they can follow fabrication, abrasion, and repair. Avoid an equal-strength brush pattern over every surface: that erases material differences and makes scale ambiguous.

**Control the amount of detail.** Establish silhouette and large masses first, then medium structure, then a few small accents. Dense grain belongs where it contributes at the actual viewing distance. Quiet surfaces help a focal object remain legible.

**Retain material distinctions under painterly treatment.** Basalt, dust, rusted iron, steel, ceramic, glass, and shell should respond differently. Painting can simplify each response without making every surface equally matte, glossy, or outlined. Do not assume a painterly art style requires abandoning the existing lit-material workflow.

**Make lighting support survival decisions.** Long shadows and brighter sunset through deeper twilight are already part of the setting. Use them to express depth and direction while keeping Booter, the Legger, paths, and interactable objects readable. A possible test palette uses iron and rust midtones, quieter dark rock, and small controlled accents; this is a proposal, not a new color standard.

**Let wear reveal use.** Prefer directional abrasion, protected recesses, repair, contact, and load-bearing relationships over uniform grunge. Corrosion treatment must follow the world's material rules; terrestrial wet-weather staining is not an automatic explanation.

**Judge the whole image.** Characters, ground, rocks, props, lighting, atmosphere, and VFX need compatible levels of simplification. A highly painted prop on noisy photographic ground may look pasted into another style. Review ground contact and the background immediately around the actor as carefully as the actor itself.

These principles are Sable's synthesis for the requested direction. They do not claim to reproduce a proprietary shader or establish that one of the references uses this exact recipe.

## Resources to bring into the workflow

Begin with the free primary material: Fortiche's departmental interviews, Moon's direct production interview, Romain Durand's development pages, official Ori imagery, and the Dishonored gallery. Then use the painting sources to study why the images work.

For practical work, the catalogue includes Krita documentation, Blender texture-paint documentation, Marco Bucci's painting education, James Gurney's Color and Light, and the studios' artist portfolios. These are reference and learning options; no tool installation or purchase is part of this task.

Poly Haven and ambientCG provide material assets under CC0 according to their published license pages. Use selected rock, dust, metal, or lighting sources as raw study material, then author them toward the brief. A photographic material library does not itself provide a coherent painted art style. [Poly Haven license](https://polyhaven.com/license), [ambientCG license](https://docs.ambientcg.com/license/).

## First comparison to make

Compare three treatments of the same small scene: a grounded lit-material baseline, the same forms with restrained painted textures, and a more visibly painted alternative. Keep composition, geometry, camera, lighting, and exposure equal during the texture comparison; compare lighting separately afterward. Include existing rock, ground, a small metal or ceramic prop, and Booter or the Legger for scale.

The [comparison brief](COMPARISON_STUDY.md) specifies the views and decisions. It is a proposed next art task; this research does not change or overwrite the user's current camp, terrain, character, or rock work.

## Research evidence and limits

Source pages were researched on October 15, 2026. The catalogue distinguishes direct studio or artist accounts, published interviews, conference descriptions, and preview-only resources. Full books and videos were not reviewed end to end, and studio rendering pipelines were not reproduced.

Background browser inspection covered Wayfinder's early environment examples, the elevated woodland image in Moon's interview, Fortiche's small artbook spread previews, and Ori's official Screenshot 14. The Moon image showed a bright traversable strip against darker surrounding masses; Ori's image showed a small luminous actor against layered dark forms. Arcane previews support broad design comparison only, not close inspection of texture maps. Dishonored's article text was available; its larger art images were age-gated in the browser and were not inspected.

No game asset, Unity setting, package, source-art file, or runtime system was changed. World identity, terrain streaming, generated-object IDs, and persisted deltas are not applicable to the research deliverables because they create no world content or runtime behavior.
