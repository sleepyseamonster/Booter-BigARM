# Realism references: Diablo IV and Path of Exile

Sable, October 15, 2026. Research requested by the author after selecting B's ground and a balance between B and C for rocks and props in the [comparison study](STUDY_RESULTS.md).

**Recommendation:** strengthen physical construction, material response, and the reasons for wear beneath the painted finish. Keep B's quiet ground and C's broad painted accents on selected rock and prop surfaces. These are proposed refinements; this research does not supersede the author's accepted treatment or change production content.

## What the references contribute

**Diablo IV is the strongest bridge between realism and painting.** Blizzard describes believability through materials and construction, physically based lighting, classical painting influences, and controlled detail at the gameplay camera. That supports our direction: realistic form and material identity can coexist with deliberate simplification. Its arid environment discussion also makes oxidized hues and dust useful reference subjects. [Blizzard environment update, March 2022](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23788294/diablo-iv-quarterly-updatemarch-2022).

Blizzard's graphics article describes physically based material inputs and environment shaders that can vary color and roughness across edges and cavities. My application is to distinguish the painted color design from the surface's response to light: a broad painted patch need not imply a glossy or deeply embossed patch. This is an art requirement, not a request to reproduce Blizzard's renderer. [Official graphics article](https://news.blizzard.com/en-gb/article/23964183/peeling-back-the-varnish-the-graphics-of-diablo-iv).

**Original Path of Exile adds useful references for material structure and assembled environments.** Its official overview identifies a gritty, realistic direction. In GGG's environment interview, Blake discusses replacing greybox modules, hiding repetition at joins, and producing intact and damaged variants. The useful lesson here is continuity and variation in authored forms; its generated levels do not prescribe our terrain architecture. [Game overview](https://www.pathofexile.com/game), [GGG environment artist interview, February 2017](https://www.pathofexile.com/forum/view-thread/1833323).

**Path of Exile 2 is a separately labelled companion reference.** Stan Brown's Act 2 material gallery provides close surface previews and contextual images. The first preview shows strong directional striations across red and gray stone-like surfaces. I would study the coherence of those directions before borrowing the density of small detail. A material preview alone cannot establish how much relief or contrast is appropriate for our gameplay camera. [Stan Brown, Act 2 materials](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/3qkzJv).

## Translation into our rock, ground, and prop

| Subject | Realism to strengthen | Painted treatment to preserve | Review question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rock | Coherent fracture, supported mass, chipped edges with thickness, plausible scale of grain | Broad color changes following major planes; selected warm and cool accents; quieter fine detail | Does it read as stone before its brushwork becomes noticeable? |
| Ground | Fine material accumulating around larger stones, convincing object contact, variation across broad patches | B's restrained color variation and low visual competition | Can Booter and small usable objects remain clear across the surface? |
| Metal prop parts | Wall thickness, fasteners, joints, exposed substrate where contact would occur, distinct roughness | Broad oxidation and abrasion accents with selected crisp edges | Can we explain each major wear area through handling, impact, exposure, or assembly? |
| Ceramic prop body | Lip and base thickness, a coherent glaze, chips exposing a distinct body material | Broad restrained glaze variation and selective brush character | Does it remain distinguishable from stone and metal under the same light? |
| Whole scene | Consistent scale, contact shadows, visible material response, clear illumination of playable forms | Composed light and selective edge/detail emphasis | Does the scene remain legible without making every surface equally busy? |

These are recommended art criteria, not measurements extracted from the games. Avoid treating extra grain, universal edge wear, crushed shadows, or glossy highlights as automatic realism. Choose a cause and a readable scale for every addition.

The current study gives us three specific refinement targets:

1. **Prop wear:** the canister's broad variation is too uniform to communicate how it was made or handled. Use construction and contact to place the major accents.
2. **Rock finish:** preserve C's larger marks while making UV transitions less conspicuous and orienting the marks to the rock's planes.
3. **Ground and lighting:** retain B's restraint, reduce visible repetition, and use an exposure where the accepted differences remain assessable. The darkest twilight comparison hides too much to settle material quality alone.

If another comparison is requested, keep geometry, lighting, wear placement, and approximate average color/value consistent while varying painted simplification. Review neutral light first, then twilight, from gameplay distance and an orbit. That would separate brush treatment from changes in damage, brightness, and illumination. It is a proposed study, not an authorized production handoff.

## Scope and handoff

The author requested more inspiration, including Diablo IV and Path of Exile. This document adds research and Sable's recommendations. Gothic iconography, gore, forest palettes, plants, natural free water, and rainfall are not additions to the game's world rules. Orange-sky twilight and the barren survival setting remain controlled by canonical documents.

No Unity assets, materials, scenes, packages, terrain, Blender sources, or production settings changed. Deterministic world identity, streaming, generated-object identity, authored placement constraints, and persisted runtime deltas are not applicable to these documentation-only deliverables because they create no world content or runtime behavior. Any later implementation needs its own bounded brief and the established Blender/Babineaux handoff.
