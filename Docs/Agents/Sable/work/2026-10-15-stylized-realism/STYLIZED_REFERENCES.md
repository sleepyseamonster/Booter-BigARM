# Stylized references led by Risk of Rain 2

Sable, October 15, 2026. The author requested more stylized inspiration, naming Risk of Rain 2, after the [realism research](REALISM_REFERENCES.md).

**Recommendation:** explore stronger shape design, broader color grouping, and selective surface detail alongside the accepted painted treatment. Risk of Rain 2 offers useful lessons in how much a scene can communicate through large forms. Its Iron Alluvium concept and finished-level references are especially relevant to our canyon-and-machinery setting. This research adds options; the accepted B ground and B–C rocks and props remain the current direction.

## Risk of Rain 2

My visual reading of the official store's desert combat still is that broad ochre ground and dark distant masses give bright actors and effects a clear stage. A second still, showing a blue structure and circular platform, uses large geometric forms and cool background masses around the brighter action. These observations concern the selected images, not every stage or moment in the game. [Official screenshot gallery](https://store.steampowered.com/app/632360/Risk_of_Rain_2/).

The most useful lessons are:

- **Design the large forms first.** Give cliffs, machines, and props recognizable profiles and plane changes before relying on surface grain.
- **Group color over space.** Let a broad terrain region share a coherent color family, then use smaller changes to describe contact and local material differences.
- **Reserve visual emphasis.** Quiet ground can support brighter usable objects, hazards, and characters. A busy combat image also shows why simultaneous bright accents need careful limits.
- **Use scale contrast.** Small figures against large cliffs and machinery can communicate vulnerability without adding decorative complexity everywhere.

These are Sable's proposed applications, not a verified reconstruction of Hopoo's shaders. Terms such as cel shading, flat shading, low-poly geometry, painted textures, and color grading describe different choices; a screenshot alone does not prove which implementation produced every visible edge or band.

### Iron Alluvium as the closest setting reference

Sean Cruz's opening concept shows a massive cylindrical machine embedded in warm cliffs, directional painted streaks across the rock, scattered simple stones, and broad ground patches. Its caption identifies the image as foundational key art made before blockouts. It is concept art, not a gameplay capture. [Sean Cruz's Iron Alluvium gallery](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/x313ZR).

Valentin Nadolu's finished-level overview shows olive/ochre rock masses and machinery framing a pale warm opening, with a small player figure establishing scale. His project account credits Cruz's concepts and blockout, describes his own final art and implementation work, and identifies Robert Deleanu's shared work on the large boring machine. This is **Alloyed Collective expansion work**, not evidence of the original release's exact asset pipeline. [Nadolu's environment gallery and credits](https://vrntech.artstation.com/projects/Z0gg9Z).

For Greater Wasteland, I would borrow the relationship between geology, enormous machinery, and human scale. Painted marks should strengthen the rock planes; small hardware detail belongs where it explains assembly or use. The reference's vegetation and other world content do not override our [world basis](../../../../WORLD_BASIS.md).

## Related styles to compare

The assessments below are Sable's art-direction readings. Similar visual traits do not establish that the developers used the same process.

| Reference | Useful lesson | Proposed place in our research |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Risk of Rain 2** | Broad forms, selective detail, grouped color, strong actor/effect accents | Main reference for simplifying the scene while preserving scale and readable action |
| **Solar Ash** | Sweeping silhouettes, large color fields, strong separation across distance | Explore the clearest, most simplified end of the range; retain our grounded construction and movement |
| **Deep Rock Galactic** | Clearly faceted rock, angular silhouette breaks, chunky equipment, strong localized light | Compare how much geometric simplification can carry geology and industrial objects |
| **Hyper Light Breaker** | Additional 3D reference within Heart Machine's Hyper Light universe | Discovery reference for further palette and silhouette study; imagery was not closely inspected in this batch |

Solar Ash's official gallery previews show orange layered ruins and a pale sloping surface against deep blue space. They are useful for broad color and scale comparisons, not close texture analysis. Creative director Alx Preston describes the game's focus on large scale and fluid movement; that movement fantasy differs from Booter's deliberate survival. [Official gallery](https://www.playstation.com/en-ph/games/solar-ash/), [Preston's developer introduction](https://blog.playstation.com/?p=346642).

Deep Rock Galactic's official `DRG_Screenshot_001.png` shows visibly faceted pink rock columns and cave walls, angular rubble, bulky equipment, and bright local effects against a darker recess. This makes it a useful geometry comparison. Its group scene is promotional gameplay imagery, not proof of our elevated gameplay-camera readability or a target for our combat effects. [Official site and screenshot gallery](https://www.deeprockgalactic.com/).

Heart Machine's FAQ establishes Hyper Light Breaker as a 3D game in the Hyper Light universe. Use it as a discovery source; the page contains older forward-looking release statements and should not be used to infer current development status. [Studio FAQ](https://www.heartmachine.com/hlb-faqs).

## Applying the research to the existing study

| Subject | Proposed stylization experiment | What to preserve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rock | Consolidate small visual breaks into fewer readable planes; align broad painted accents with fracture direction | Recognizable stone, believable scale, and the selected B–C surface character |
| Ground | Group existing variation into broader quiet patches and concentrate stones near meaningful formations | B's accepted subtle treatment and clear footing |
| Prop | Clarify silhouette and major component boundaries; simplify minor competing marks | Construction, functional material distinctions, and motivated wear from the realism research |
| Distant environment | Group distant forms through value and atmosphere so the nearest useful forms separate | Orange sky, rust/iron world palette, and recognizable landmark scale |
| Character and interaction accents | Test a small number of stronger value/color accents against quieter surroundings | Grounded survival tone and a clear distinction between interactables, hazards, and decoration |

The strongest combination to explore is **believable construction, designed large forms, and selective painted detail**. Realism and stylization can be adjusted independently: we can simplify a rock's visible detail while retaining convincing fracture, or clarify a machine's silhouette while keeping credible joints and thickness.

For a future controlled comparison, first keep the same geometry and lighting and reduce competing texture detail around the accepted treatment. A separate geometry comparison can then test broader planes and simpler silhouettes with matched materials. Separating these questions would show whether we prefer the surface economy, the shapes, or both. No new render or production integration is part of this research batch.
