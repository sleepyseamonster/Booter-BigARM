Its approximately 9.05 × 7.37 metre footprint retains the authored arrangement and the existing white limestone and gray base colors.

## Geometry and surface

Each stone is transformed into metre coordinates on a copy, clipped 10 mm below the plane, capped, and simplified against its own original shape. Candidate budgets must retain at least 99.3% overlap of the sampled top silhouette, with sampled bidirectional surface distance at most 8 mm and its 95th percentile at most 3 mm. The plane becomes a solid 25 mm base extending downward. The exact Boolean union removes internal surfaces and produces one connected mesh. A further measured reduction of the fused mesh uses the same distance limits; it does not treat invisible internal faces as useful detail.

The 5,500-triangle, 2,752-vertex native mesh is the smallest tested fused budget that passed those limits, compared with 16,376 original stone triangles plus the plane's two triangles. This is approximately 66.4% fewer triangles despite adding a closed base. It is a measured artistic compromise, not a claim of a mathematically absolute minimum. Sampling and silhouette thresholds have finite resolution; final visual review and direct source comparisons remain part of acceptance.

A separate 128 × 128 top-surface comparison per stone measured at least 99.35% footprint overlap against the untouched original assembly. The maximum sampled nearest-surface distance was 6.98 mm; the largest per-stone 95th percentile was 3.09 mm across the combined reduction steps. Vertical ray differences at steep outline transitions can equal the stone height even when a small horizontal outline shift causes the transition; those differences are recorded separately and are not substituted for geometric nearest-surface distance.

The final surface uses one 4K UV atlas and one material. Existing source colors, roughness and normals are baked from the preserved original stones and solid base. Contact AO uses a 0.2 metre artistic radius. BaseColor is sRGB; Normal, Occlusion and MetallicSmoothness are linear data. MetallicSmoothness contains zero metallic RGB and smoothness in alpha. The auxiliary Roughness image supports the packed Blender material and is not needed by the Unity material. Padding is 16 pixels before mip filtering; clamp sampling is intentional for an atlas.

The normal bake uses the original unclipped source stones, preserving their original corner shading rather than smoothing against artificial cut caps. Nearly-flat base faces remain flat shaded below 5 mm. The first normal-bake preview exposed dark edge artifacts, so `refine_flat_stone_patch_normals.py` repeats only the normal bake with these corrections and refreshes the FBX/cache; geometry, UVs and the completed color, roughness and AO maps remain unchanged.

The ground/contact pivot is the selected plane's centre at its upper surface. The mesh has unit scale and rotation. One Blender metre equals one Unity unit. Place the Unity root at ground height; the base extends 25 mm beneath that height. The stones project above it. Move, rotate around the vertical axis, or duplicate the single root to place patches. It is an authored patch, not an automatically seamless tile texture.

## Runtime handoff and verification

Thin-edge projection produced some inward-facing tangent normals. The normal-data cleanup falls back to geometry normals for those pixels, smoothly tapering the correction over tangent Z=0..0.25. This affects 2.58% of the atlas pixels, including padding; the remaining baked detail is unchanged.

Runtime FBX and four atlas textures: `Assets/_Project/Art/Environment/Ground/FlatStoneGroundPatch01/`.
Unity prefab: `Assets/_Project/Prefabs/TopDown3D/Environment/FlatStoneGroundPatch01.prefab`.

The prefab has one root, one MeshFilter, one MeshRenderer with the single URP Lit atlas material, and a static non-convex MeshCollider using the fused mesh. It has no Rigidbody or gameplay scripts. The importer generates secondary lightmap UVs; runtime vertex counts exceed native unique vertices because UV, shading and lightmap boundaries split vertices. Fresh Unity import has 8,131 runtime vertices and 5,500 triangles. The imported FBX node's axis transform is baked into the Runtime mesh asset, including normals and tangents, so the prefab root has identity rotation and unit scale. Imported bounds are 9.051995 × 0.176534 × 7.370174 metres, with its underside at Y=-0.0250004. It is intended for static ground dressing, with no production-scene placement performed by this task.

Reproducible tools under `Tools/Art/Blender/`: `flat_stone_patch_pipeline.py` inspects and prepares; `apply_prepared_flat_stone_patch.py` applies a reviewed preparation to the same saved scene; `verify_flat_stone_patch.py` freshly checks native geometry, UVs, packed maps, unchanged originals and FBX bounds; `compare_flat_stone_patch.py` samples the exposed source and final top surfaces; `import_flat_stone_patch.cs` performs the scoped Unity import and prefab creation through Pipeline `eval_file`.

Proof lives in ignored `Logs/FlatStoneGroundPatch01/`: reduction audit, preparation, renders, native/FBX verification, direct surface comparison, and Unity import receipt. `application.json` records the saved-scene hashes, retained source signatures, mesh facts and export hashes once application completes. Structural checks cover closed connected topology, positive volume, no loose or zero-area faces, non-adjacent segment/triangle crossings, usable UVs, one material, FBX scale/pivot and collider cooking.
